Most Valley neighborhoods where people keep horses do it on sufferance — a legacy use the zoning tolerates. Granada Hills is different. Horse-keeping here sits inside a supplemental use district the City adopted by ordinance, with boundaries and standards on paper. That single fact shapes lot sizes, what your neighbor can do, and who competes with you for a property.

The Long Story

A community that chose, on paper, not to change quickly.

Plenty of neighborhoods say they want to preserve their character. Granada Hills wrote it down and passed it into law. The 2015 plan update is explicitly a conservative-growth plan — aimed at preserving single-family neighborhoods, hillsides and open space — and it came with the ordinances to back it.

“A ranch that became a park. A custom that became an ordinance.”

The ranch era C.J. Ranch — citrus and cattle

Before the park and before the tracts, the northern edge of Granada Hills was working ranch land: citrus groves and cattle running up against the Santa Susana foothills. The terrain that makes the north end feel different today is the same terrain that made it ranch land then.

1976 O’Melveny Park is deeded to the City

Attorney John O’Melveny deeded the former ranch property at 17300 Sesnon Boulevard to the City of Los Angeles. At 672 acres it became — and remains — the second-largest park in the city. Most buyers looking at Granada Hills have no idea the open space at the top of the neighborhood is that large.

2015 The Community Plan update

The City adopted the updated Granada Hills–Knollwood Community Plan, described in the record as a conservative-growth plan aimed at preserving single-family neighborhoods, hillsides and open space. A community plan is the document that governs what land can become; this one deliberately chose continuity.

2015 Three ordinances give it teeth

Council approved three implementing ordinances alongside the plan: the Granada Hills Specific Plan, the boundaries and standards of the Equinekeeping “K” Supplemental Use District, and a Residential Floor Area district covering Old Granada Hills. A plan without ordinances is a wish. These are the rules.

Mar. 24, 2025 New fire hazard maps for the city

CAL FIRE and the Office of the State Fire Marshal released updated Local Responsibility Area maps covering the City of Los Angeles — the first update in over a decade, and the first to map three tiers inside city limits. The update added more than 440,000 acres to Los Angeles County’s hazard zones. See the fire section below for what that changes for a buyer here.

Ground Truth

What locals know that listings don’t say.

Horse-keeping is a zoning right here, not a neighborly arrangement

The Equinekeeping “K” Supplemental Use District has adopted boundaries and standards. If keeping horses matters to you, the question is not whether the lot looks big enough — it is whether the parcel sits inside the district and meets its standards. Confirm that before you write an offer, not after.

Two ZIPs, one neighborhood

Granada Hills is covered by 91344 and by 91394, a branch-post-office ZIP enclosed within it. That split quietly fragments ZIP-level market data, so a “91344 median” is not describing the whole neighborhood. Ask which parcels are actually in the set.

Old Granada Hills has its own floor-area rules

One of the 2015 ordinances established a Residential Floor Area district covering Old Granada Hills. If your plan involves adding square footage, that district governs how much you can build — a detail that decides whether a value-add purchase pencils.

The high school is a charter, and that changes the question

Granada Hills Charter serves over 6,000 students across a high school campus on Zelzah Avenue and a TK–8 campus on Devonshire. Because it is a charter rather than a boundary-assigned school, “is this house in the zone?” is the wrong question. Admission works differently — ask the school directly rather than assuming an address guarantees a seat.

The open space at the top is not a greenbelt

O’Melveny Park at 17300 Sesnon is 672 acres — second largest in the City of Los Angeles. Bee Canyon Park sits nearby at 13150 Sesnon with playgrounds and trails. This is real hiking terrain, and it is also wildland, which is exactly why the fire question below deserves a parcel-level answer.

The plan chose slow growth deliberately

The 2015 community plan is on record as conservative-growth, aimed at preserving single-family neighborhoods, hillsides and open space. Whether that is good news depends on which side of the transaction you are on — it constrains new supply, and it also protects what you bought.

The Numbers

How to read the Granada Hills market like a local.

Equestrian parcels are their own market

A property inside the equinekeeping district and meeting its standards competes for a different buyer than an identical-looking house outside it. Comparing the two as if they were substitutes will mislead you in both directions. I price them separately, and I will show you which set I used.

Two ZIPs distort the median

With 91394 nested inside 91344, ZIP-level statistics blend sub-markets. Insist on comparables drawn from actual streets and actual product type rather than a ZIP aggregate.

Supply is plan-constrained by design

A conservative-growth community plan plus a floor-area district in Old Granada Hills is a structural constraint on how much housing gets added and how large existing homes can become. That belongs in any honest long-run conversation.

Price insurance before you price the house

What I actually know about Granada Hills.

The ordinances, the districts, and the details that decide whether a parcel here can do what you want it to do.

A plan with ordinances behind it

The City adopted the updated Granada Hills–Knollwood Community Plan in 2015, and Council approved three implementing ordinances alongside it: the Granada Hills Specific Plan, the Equinekeeping “K” Supplemental Use District boundaries and standards, and a Residential Floor Area district for Old Granada Hills. The plan is on record as a conservative-growth plan aimed at preserving single-family neighborhoods, hillsides and open space.

Horse-keeping is codified, not tolerated

The Equinekeeping “K” Supplemental Use District is an adopted zoning overlay with defined boundaries and standards. In most of Los Angeles, keeping a horse is a legacy use you hope survives. Here it is written into the code — which means the real question about any parcel is whether it falls inside the district, not whether the lot looks big enough.

Old Granada Hills has its own size limits

One of the 2015 ordinances established a Residential Floor Area district covering Old Granada Hills. If your plan involves adding square footage or building larger, that district governs how much. Check it before you price a value-add strategy, because it can decide whether the numbers work at all.

Two ZIP codes, one neighborhood

Granada Hills is covered by 91344 and by 91394, a branch-post-office ZIP enclosed within 91344. Because of that split, ZIP-level market statistics describe a fragment rather than the neighborhood. Any “91344 median” deserves the question: which parcels are actually in that set?

The high school is a charter

Granada Hills Charter is an independent charter affiliated with LAUSD, serving over 6,000 students across a high school campus on Zelzah Avenue and a TK–8 campus on Devonshire. U.S. News ranks the high school #114 in California and #880 nationally, with a 68% AP participation rate. The second-largest park in the city

Local brief · Granada Hills
    Straight answers

    Fire zones and insurance: the straight version.

    The north end of Granada Hills runs into real wildland, and the maps changed in 2025. Here is what is actually true, and how to get a real answer for a specific address.

    I will not tell you “Granada Hills is in X zone.” I will show you how to check the parcel.

    Fire hazard severity is mapped geographically, parcel by parcel — not by neighborhood and not by ZIP. In a community that runs from valley floor up into 672 acres of open space, that distinction is not academic: two homes a few streets apart can carry different designations. Look the address up on the CAL FIRE / Office of the State Fire Marshal hazard zone viewer, then confirm against the Los Angeles Fire Department Fire Zone Map and the city’s ZIMAS parcel system before removing a contingency.

    Three tiers where there used to be one

    On March 24, 2025, CAL FIRE and the Office of the State Fire Marshal released updated Local Responsibility Area maps covering the City of Los Angeles — the first update in over a decade. They map three tiers: Moderate, High and Very High. Earlier city maps showed only Very High, so Moderate and High are categories that did not previously exist inside city limits. A parcel newly showing “Moderate” has not been downgraded.

    The update was substantial

    The 2025 maps added more than 440,000 acres to Los Angeles County’s hazard zones, including roughly a 30% increase in acreage at the highest severity rating. If you last checked a parcel here before 2025, that answer may simply be out of date.

    A city can raise a zone, never lower it

    State law lets a local agency increase a state-recommended hazard level but not decrease it, and Los Angeles is required to adopt the recommended maps by ordinance. Confirm the current adopted status rather than assuming — LAFD was still aligning the state boundaries to city streets and parcel lines as of the most recent public record I could verify.

    What a Very High or High designation actually triggers

    Buildings in those zones must comply with the California Wildland-Urban Interface Code as amended by the Los Angeles Municipal Code, and VHFHSZ parcels carry specific building and brush-clearance obligations under LAMC sections 57.4911 and 91.7207. These are real construction and maintenance requirements, not paperwork — and they are the first thing an insurer’s inspector looks for.

    Defensible space is already the law

    California Public Resources Code 4291 requires 100 feet of defensible space around structures in these areas. That one is in effect now, and it is the first thing an insurer's inspector looks at.

    “Zone 0” is coming, but is not law yet

    The proposed ember-resistant zone — the first five feet around a house, kept clear of anything that burns — was created by AB 3074 in 2020 and reshaped by SB 504 (2024), AB 1455 (2025) and Executive Order N-18-25. The Board of Forestry's April 17, 2026 draft proposes phased implementation and is still a draft. Anyone telling you Zone 0 is already mandatory statewide is ahead of the record.

    The FAIR Plan is a backstop, not a bargain

    It is an involuntary pool shared by every insurer licensed to write basic property insurance in California — not a state agency, and not funded by taxpayers. Its residential exposure grew 424% between September 2020 and June 2025, reaching $603 billion, with roughly 573,700 policies in force as of March 2025. It is the insurer of last resort, and it is priced like it.

    The rules for insurers changed too

    Under the Department of Insurance's Sustainable Insurance Strategy, the wildfire catastrophe-modeling regulation was announced December 13, 2024, with reinsurance and ratemaking regulations submitted for approval December 30, 2024. Insurers gained the ability to use catastrophe models in setting rates in exchange for commitments to write in distressed wildfire areas.

    Sources: CAL FIRE / Office of the State Fire Marshal fire hazard severity zone maps (March 24, 2025 Los Angeles County release); California Public Resources Code 4291; California Board of Forestry and Fire Protection Zone 0 rulemaking record; California FAIR Plan; California Department of Insurance Sustainable Insurance Strategy. Zone designations and insurance availability change — verify at the parcel and in writing before removing contingencies. Educational information, not insurance advice.

    Common questions

    Granada Hills, answered.

    Can I keep horses in Granada Hills?

    In parts of it, yes — and unusually for Los Angeles, it is codified rather than merely tolerated. The Equinekeeping “K” Supplemental Use District, whose boundaries and standards Council adopted by ordinance in 2015, governs where and how. The right question about any specific property is whether the parcel sits inside that district and meets its standards, not whether the lot looks large enough. Confirm before you write an offer.

    What fire hazard severity zone is Granada Hills in?

    There is no single answer for the neighborhood, and that matters here more than most places because Granada Hills runs from valley floor up into hundreds of acres of open space. Zones are mapped parcel by parcel from terrain, fuels, weather and fire history. Look the specific address up on the CAL FIRE / Office of the State Fire Marshal viewer and confirm against the LAFD Fire Zone Map and ZIMAS.

    What changed in the March 2025 fire hazard maps?

    On March 24, 2025 CAL FIRE and the Office of the State Fire Marshal released updated Local Responsibility Area maps for the City of Los Angeles, the first in more than a decade. They map three tiers — Moderate, High and Very High — where city maps previously mapped only Very High, and the update added more than 440,000 acres to the county’s hazard zones, including roughly a 30% increase at the highest severity rating.

    What ZIP codes cover Granada Hills?

    Two: 91344, and 91394, which is a branch-post-office ZIP enclosed within 91344. That split is worth knowing because it fragments ZIP-level market data — statistics quoted for one ZIP are describing part of the neighborhood rather than all of it.

    What high school serves Granada Hills?

    Granada Hills Charter, an independent charter affiliated with LAUSD, serving over 6,000 students across a high school campus on Zelzah Avenue and a TK–8 campus on Devonshire. U.S. News ranks the high school #114 in California and #880 nationally, with 68% AP participation. Because it is a charter rather than a boundary-assigned school, buying an address does not by itself secure a seat — confirm admission directly with the school.

    Is there much new construction in Granada Hills?

    Structurally, less than in many Valley neighborhoods, and that is deliberate. The 2015 Granada Hills–Knollwood Community Plan is on record as a conservative-growth plan aimed at preserving single-family neighborhoods, hillsides and open space, and a Residential Floor Area district limits how large homes in Old Granada Hills can become. That constrains new supply and also protects what you buy.

    How big is O’Melveny Park?

    672 acres — the second-largest park in the City of Los Angeles, at 17300 Sesnon Boulevard. Attorney John O’Melveny deeded the former C.J. Ranch citrus and cattle property to the City in 1976. Bee Canyon Park is nearby at 13150 Sesnon with playgrounds and hiking trails.
