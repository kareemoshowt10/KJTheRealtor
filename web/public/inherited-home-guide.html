01
Before anyone clears anything

I secure what matters first

My objective: nothing gets thrown, donated, or sold until the house has been photographed room by room and the paper trail is pulled. Grief already makes this hard — an irreversible mistake in week one makes it worse. Slow down the first 48 hours so the rest can move fast.
Secure before anything moves

Documents & anything that photographs the house as-is

The will or trust, the property tax bill, homeowners insurance, any prior appraisal, deeds, and a full photo walkthrough of every room before a single box is opened. This is your record — for the county, for the IRS, and for your own memory.

Can wait until the family has agreed

Everything else in the house

Furniture, clothing, keepsakes, the garage, decades of accumulated belongings. None of it needs a decision this week. The instinct to "just start dealing with it" is exactly what creates fights and regret later.

Why photos before anythingA full-house photo walkthrough does two things: it's your record if a step-up-in-basis appraisal ever needs to describe the home's condition at death, and it means nobody has to rely on memory later for "wasn't there a [item] in that room?"
Powerful ally · Slow down first
The fastest path through this starts by not rushing the first week.

Families who secure the paperwork and photograph the house before touching anything move faster overall — because nobody has to backtrack, second-guess a donation, or fight over what "used to be there."

Call 1 · I call an estate clean-out company

I turn "how do we clear it" into a number

What you are watching: a guess about clean-out cost keeps families stuck for months. A real walkthrough estimate from a licensed company turns "we should probably clear it out eventually" into an actual plan with an actual price — the same evidence-before-decisions habit I use in every negotiation.
Kareem ↔ Estate clean-out companyScoping the job
Why a walkthrough, not a phone quoteClean-out pricing swings enormously with access, stairs, and whether items need to be sorted or just hauled. A number given without seeing the house isn't a bid — it's a guess with a dollar sign on it.
Hi, I'm a Realtor working with a family on an inherited property. The house is [size] and hasn't been cleared in a while — [volume] of belongings. Can you do a walkthrough and give me a real bid, not a phone estimate?
EC
Absolutely — we never quote sight-unseen. Based on what you're describing, full-house clean-outs like this usually run $[low] to $[high], but I'll confirm after I walk it.
Two questions before we book it: can the family pull items aside first and have you haul the rest, and do you handle donation drop-off separately from the landfill haul?
EC
Yes to both. Most families take a weekend to pull keepsakes first — we work around whatever's flagged, and we route usable furniture and clothing to donation before anything goes to the dump.
That's what I want to hear. Can you put the walkthrough bid in writing with a firm date, so the family can plan the sort weekend around it?
Why I ask about donation routingA company that separates donation from landfill is doing the sorting work the family would otherwise have to do themselves — worth more than a slightly lower price from a company that just hauls everything to a dumpster.

Call 2 · I call the client's sister

I get everyone aligned before anything moves

How I open: this isn't a negotiation, it's a conversation designed to prevent one — the fights I've watched families have aren't usually about the house, they're about one sibling feeling decisions got made without them. My job is to make sure that never happens.
Kareem ↔ Client's sister & co-heirGetting everyone on the same page
Why I loop in the sibling myselfWhen one heir does all the coordinating alone, the other often feels shut out — even with good intentions on both sides. A short call from me, not just a text from [client], usually resets the whole tone.
Hi [name], I know this is a hard time. I'm helping [sibling] think through the house — not deciding anything, just making sure nothing happens without you. Is now an okay time for five minutes?
SIB
Okay — honestly I've been dreading this conversation.
That's completely normal, and nothing has to be decided today. Here's what I'd suggest: the whole family walks the house together one time, everyone flags what they want to keep with a sticky note, we photograph it all first — then the clean-out company handles the rest on a date everyone agrees to.
SIB
What if we want different things, or someone's not free that weekend?
Anyone who can't make it gets a video call walkthrough of the rooms first — nobody flags anything sight-unseen, and nobody's excluded for a scheduling conflict. If two people want the same item, that's a family decision, not something I weigh in on — I just make sure it's raised before the truck shows up, not after.
Why I stay out of "who gets what"Deciding which sibling gets an item isn't a real estate question, and it isn't mine to answer. My role is making sure the process is fair and nothing happens by surprise — the family decides the rest.

04
The feelings that come up

I keep the plan moving, gently

What you are watching: every reaction below is one I've actually heard, and none of them means the plan is wrong. Grief, guilt, and overwhelm are the normal texture of this process — I acknowledge each one and hand back a small, doable next step, not a bigger ask.
SIB
I'm not ready to let go of any of it yet.
You don't have to be — today's only decision is scheduling one walkthrough, not clearing the house. Keep-and-decide-later is a completely valid box for anything you're not ready for.
SIB
Can't we just sell it as-is and skip all of this?
Genuinely, sometimes yes — I'll show you the real numbers on both paths in the next step. But even selling as-is, I'd still want the photo walkthrough and document pull done first. That part protects you either way.
SIB
What if there's something valuable in there we don't know about?
Fair concern, and common. Before any clean-out crew touches a room, I always recommend one careful pass through drawers, closets, and anywhere paper or jewelry tends to hide — that's exactly what the family walkthrough in step three is for.
SIB
My sibling wants everything gone yesterday and it's stressing me out.
That tension is really common — one person processes by moving fast, the other needs time. I'll say the same thing to both of you: nothing gets scheduled until you've both agreed on a date. Speed for one person doesn't get to override the other's pace.
Powerful ally · Patience is the strategy
Nobody has ever regretted taking one extra week on this. People regret rushing it.

My job in this step isn't to get the house cleared faster — it's to make sure the pace feels survivable to everyone involved, because that's what actually prevents the process from stalling out entirely.

05
Nothing is scheduled until it's agreed

We choose the path, together

The step families skip: deciding "clear it out" or "sell as-is" by default, under deadline pressure, instead of on purpose. Both paths are legitimate. The only mistake is not actually choosing — letting exhaustion make the decision for you.
Kareem ↔ ClientPlan confirmed
Here's where things stand: the clean-out bid came back at $[amount], your family walkthrough is set for [date], and I ran the numbers on selling as-is versus clearing first. As-is nets you roughly $[amount-as-is] with no work on your end. Clearing first and listing costs $[amount] and [X] weeks, but typically nets $[amount-cleared] after the clean-out expense. Which fits your family better?
CL
Honestly, none of us have the bandwidth to manage a clean-out right now. Let's talk about as-is.
That's a completely valid call — plenty of buyers specifically look for as-is estate properties, and I'll price and market it that way honestly, not hide the condition and hope nobody notices. We still do the family walkthrough first, unhurried, before any offer goes out.
Why I never push one pathThe "right" answer depends entirely on the family's time, energy, and finances — not on which path nets a few thousand dollars more. I show the real numbers on both, and the family chooses the life that fits, not just the spreadsheet.
AS-IS

Sell as-is

No clean-out, no staging, no weeks of work. Priced and marketed honestly to buyers who specifically want an estate property — often faster to close, sometimes a lower net.

CLEAR-FIRST

Clear, then list

Full clean-out and often light updates before listing. More upfront cost and time, typically the strongest net proceeds — the right call when the family has the bandwidth.

PARTIAL

Partial sort

Family pulls keepsakes and anything appraisal-worthy; the clean-out crew handles the rest; the house lists as-is minus what was removed. The middle path most families actually choose.

Powerful ally · Your decision, informed
My job is to make the decision clearer — not to make it for you.

Every path above is one I've walked a family through and watched work. You choose from real numbers and a real plan, on your own timeline — not from panic, and not from a stranger's default.
